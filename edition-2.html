01 / The business model

What if access
beats ownership?

Rentomojo rents furniture and home appliances on subscription. Instead of a large upfront purchase, customers pay for use and can adapt their setup as life changes. The business has to make convenience work across logistics, maintenance, refurbishment and repeat deployment.

The customer problem

Furniture is useful. Owning it can be inconvenient.

Students, young professionals and people relocating may need a furnished home without wanting to commit capital, transport bulky items or manage resale. Rentomojo sells flexibility and convenience—not just a sofa or washing machine.

The operating challenge

One asset. Multiple earning cycles.

Unlike a conventional retailer, a rental platform must manage the asset after the first customer: collection, inspection, repair, refurbishment and redeployment. Asset utilisation and useful life can be as important as customer acquisition.

03 / Learning & development

Think like an
operator-investor.

For subscription businesses, headline growth can hide operational complexity. Use this quick checklist to analyse whether growth is durable and whether each asset earns enough over its lifetime.

Four metrics worth tracking

Look beyond revenue.

  • Utilisation: what share of rentable assets is earning revenue?
  • Payback period: how long until an asset recovers its cost?
  • Repeat / retention: do customers renew or rent again?
  • Contribution margin: what remains after delivery, service, repair and other variable costs?
